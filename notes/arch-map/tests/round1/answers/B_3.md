## B3-01
FILES: access/nbtree/nbtpage.c, access/nbtree/nbtxlog.c, access/nbtree/nbtree.c, access/nbtree/nbtsearch.c, access/nbtree/nbtinsert.c
ANSWER: The reworked deletion does not hold the leaf lock for the whole of phase two. The leaf is unlocked and relocked while the chain is unlinked top to bottom. Meanwhile the sibling chain around the half-dead leaf can change (the right sibling, or the left sibling, may be split or deleted). The unlink step then used stale sibling/parent assumptions, or WAL replay of the multi-page record did not mirror the locking order. Result: the leaf's left/right links are inconsistent after the unlink, or the half-dead page is not recognised, so the page can't be deleted or the sibling chain is broken. This breaks Lehman-Yao sibling-link consistency and concurrent scan/replay safety. (Inferred, not in the README.)

## B3-02

ANSWER: heap_update only partly honoured the new "no logical logging" flag. Some WAL records written on the update path were not suppressed. Candidates: TOAST rows written by heap_toast_insert_or_update, the old-key/replica-identity part of the update record, or the INSERT-type record emitted when the tuple moves to another page. ## B3-03
FILES: access/heap/heapam.c, access/heap/heapam_visibility.c, access/transam/multixact.c
ANSWER: Fix: do not decide the status of a multixact member updater from pg_xact alone. Ask the proc array first (TransactionIdIsInProgress), then pg_xact. A member that is neither in progress nor committed is treated as aborted. Its lock-only members are still honoured, and the tuple is treated as locked rather than updated. This applies in heap_update, heap_lock_tuple and HeapTupleSatisfiesUpdate, via the multixact "is running" and "update xid" helpers. Reasoning: pg_xact is set to aborted before the proc array entry is removed, so a clog-only test races. After a crash the updater is not committed and not running, so the row is simply updatable. The state must follow the interlock of commit/abort ordering against ProcArrayEndTransaction.
CONFIDENCE: medium for (a); medium for (b)
DRILL: Read MultiXactIdIsRunning and MultiXactIdGetUpdateXid in multixact.c, and the HEAP_XMAX_IS_MULTI branches of HeapTupleSatisfiesUpdate and heap_update.

## B3-05
FILES: access/transam/xact.c, replication/logical/logicalctl.c, storage/ipc/procsignal.c, access/transam/xlog.c, replication/slot.c
ANSWER: The deferred-apply path on abort was wrong. Possible causes: it was applied at the wrong point in AbortTransaction (still in a critical or aborted state, before resources were released, or for the wrong transaction level), or a subtransaction abort applied it while the top-level XID was still assigned. Backends could then keep a stale WAL level and logical-info setting. Records would be written without the logical information that decoding needs, or with it after decoding was turned off. This violates the guarantee that a transaction's WAL level matches the effective level, and the invariant that the level is stable once an XID is assigned.

## B3-06
FILES: commands/wait.c, access/transam/xlogwait.c, storage/lmgr/lock.c, storage/lmgr/proc.c, access/transam/xlogrecovery.c
ANSWER: Restricting only replay waits is not enough. Write and flush targets are floored by the replay position, so they too depend on replay. If the WAL receiver stops before the target, recovery blocked on a lock can't restart the receiver or recycle WAL, so pg_wal fills. "A receiver is active when the wait starts" is only a snapshot. The receiver can stop or fail later, and nothing in the waiter or in recovery can restart it. Only the cheap conservative check "session already holds a granted lock" is race-free, so it was applied to every wait type. Locks are not registered as a wait on replay, so the deadlock detector can't resolve it. (Reasoning from the README on locking during Hot Standby, where the startup process can be blocked by backends' locks.)
CONFIDENCE: medium for (a); medium for (b)
DRILL: Read WaitForLSN in xlogwait.c and the new lock-held check in lock.c (loop over LOCALLOCK, nLocks > 0).

## B3-07
FILES: access/transam/twophase.c, access/transam/xact.c, storage/lmgr/proc.c
ANSWER: Make the abort path in FinishPreparedTransaction behave like the commit path. Inside a critical section, set MyProc->delayChkptFlags (DELAY_CHKPT_IN_COMMIT / start) before inserting the abort record and keep it until after pg_xact is updated (RecordTransactionAbortPrepared, then TransactionIdAbortTree), then clear it. A checkpoint therefore can't choose its redo pointer between the WAL flush and the clog update. Reason: the flag is an interlock that makes the "WAL record written" and "clog updated" steps atomic with respect to checkpoint redo selection. Otherwise recovery starts after the record and never sees the clog update.
CONFIDENCE: high for (a); high for (b)
DRILL: Compare RecordTransactionCommitPrepared and RecordTransactionAbortPrepared in twophase.c (delayChkptFlags and START_CRIT_SECTION).

## B3-08
FILES: storage/lmgr/proc.c, storage/lmgr/lock.c, storage/ipc/standby.c, utils/adt/lockfuncs.c
ANSWER: Make the waiter own its waitStart. Clear it (to 0) when the wait finishes on every exit path: in LockErrorCleanup for cancel, timeout and deadlock victim, and after the grant in ProcSleep. Stop having the waker (ProcLockWakeup / GrantLock) reset it. Store the new start time with an atomic write before the wait begins. In standby.c, the startup process (ResolveRecoveryConflictWithLock) does the same reset/store for each wait. Reason: a single writer avoids the race between the waker's reset and the waiter's new store. Every path that leaves the wait (error or grant) is covered, so there is no stale value.
CONFIDENCE: medium for (a); medium for (b)
DRILL: Read ProcSleep, LockErrorCleanup and ProcLockWakeup/GrantLock in proc.c and lock.c where pg_atomic waitStart is touched.

## B3-11
FILES: storage/aio/read_stream.c, access/heap/vacuumlazy.c, storage/buffer/freelist.c, storage/buffer/bufmgr.c
ANSWER: Recreating the stream is not safe or practical. The stream holds the block-number callback state and the current scan position, lookahead and queued buffers with their pins, and in-flight AIO (see the AIO README, where handles and completions are shared and can't be moved). A new stream would have to hand all of those over. The callback private data is owned by VACUUM's first heap pass. Pin limits are computed at creation from the strategy. A rebuild in the middle of the failsafe, an urgent path that should be minimal and robust, would add risk of losing or double-counting blocks. Dropping the strategy in place is the smaller and more reliable change, at the price of a lower pin limit.
CONFIDENCE: medium for (a); low for (b)
DRILL: Read read_stream_begin_relation (pin limit from GetAccessStrategyPinLimit) and the failsafe code in vacuumlazy.c (lazy_check_wraparound_failsafe).

## B3-12
FILES: storage/aio/method_io_uring.c, storage/aio/aio.c, storage/aio/aio_init.c, postmaster/postmaster.c, storage/ipc/ipci.c
ANSWER: Release the old rings when shared memory is reinitialised. Before the postmaster recreates AIO state after a crash, it calls io_uring_queue_exit() on each existing ring (closing the fds and unmapping the ring memory). This is done through an AIO shutdown/teardown hook called from the postmaster's reset path (reset_shared / CreateSharedMemoryAndSemaphores), or an on-exit callback. Reason: the rings are fds and kernel memory owned by the postmaster, not by shared memory, so shmem reinit doesn't free them. Without an explicit close they leak at every crash restart until EMFILE or ENOMEM. The teardown must happen in the postmaster, which created them and holds the fds, before new ones are made.
CONFIDENCE: medium for (a); medium for (b)
DRILL: Read pgaio_uring_shmem_init and the io_method ops (shmem_init, init_backend) in method_io_uring.c, and the postmaster's reinit path after a crash.
